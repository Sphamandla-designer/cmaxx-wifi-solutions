Choose controls that suit your existing lights

The right control depends on the fitting, circuit, household habits and the function you want. App control, dimming and colour changing are different capabilities, so the equipment must be chosen around the desired result.

Option 1

Smart bulbs

A compatible smart bulb can be a simple starting point for a lamp or selected fitting. Some provide dimming, adjustable white light or colour. It normally needs power available to respond, so an ordinary switch cutting power can interrupt app control. Bulb size, fitting type, enclosure suitability and the supported hub or connection must be checked.

Option 2

Smart switches and relays

A suitable smart switch or relay can provide connected control for an existing lighting circuit while preserving an appropriate wall-control method. The electrical professional checks the actual wiring, space, load and multi-way arrangement. A switching relay does not make a non-dimmable lamp dimmable or add colour control.

Option 3

Smart dimmers

Dimming requires a compatible dimmer and lighting load, including the lamp or LED driver. Incorrect combinations may perform poorly. The installer must check manufacturer guidance, supported load and operating range; CMAXX then configures the agreed smart-control functions.

Mixing devices needs deliberate design. Mixing smart bulbs and a mains-switching control on the same circuit needs deliberate design. A controller that cuts power may make the smart bulbs unavailable. Where supported, a different control arrangement can retain power while providing a usable wall interface.

Lighting should remain understandable when conditions change

A lighting setup should be tested through the agreed wall controls, apps and routines. We also explain which parts depend on a hub, local network or internet service.

During an internet outage, supported local lighting controls may continue if the required equipment remains powered. Cloud-based actions and some voice functions may not. Power loss is different: mains-powered lights and controllers need an appropriate supply, and their power-return behaviour varies.

We discuss the recovery settings available on the selected equipment, test agreed scenes and show you how to make everyday changes. The handover should include clear room names, account ownership and a practical route for support.

Where coverage is inadequate, a network improvement may be required before adding more controls. A stronger overall design is more useful than repeatedly resetting devices without understanding why they disconnect. Smart lighting installation FAQs

Can I keep my existing light fittings?

Often, suitable fittings can remain, but the control approach depends on the lamp or driver, wiring and the functions you want. A smart switch can provide supported circuit control without making every fitting dimmable. We assess the lighting and electrical requirements before recommending replacements.

Do smart switches need a neutral wire?

Some do and some are designed for different wiring arrangements. A label such as no-neutral does not make a device suitable for every circuit or load. The electrical contractor must check the actual installation and manufacturer requirements; do not assume compatibility from a product photograph.

Can my existing dimmer remain in place?

Only where the proposed combination is supported. Smart bulbs, dimmers and LED drivers may have different requirements, and placing one control behind another can cause problems. The correct solution depends on the specific equipment and wiring rather than a universal keep-or-replace rule.

Will the wall switches still work?

The design should preserve an appropriate everyday control method where supported. The behaviour depends on whether the wall control switches mains power, sends a smart command or uses a supported alternative mode. We agree and demonstrate how the household will operate the lights rather than assuming all devices behave identically.

Can lights turn on automatically when I enter a room?

Supported sensors can trigger suitable lighting. We consider detection coverage, daylight conditions and the delay before lights switch off. A presence use case may need different equipment from simple motion detection. A manual override remains useful when the room is used differently.

Can lighting work without the internet?

Some local switch controls and locally executed routines can, provided the required equipment is powered and operating. Cloud-dependent features and some voice actions may not. The exact system must be assessed and tested; offline operation is not guaranteed simply because a device is described as smart.

Can I combine Alexa or Google Home with the lighting?

Compatible products may support the required platform and functions. We verify the specific integration, account region and commands, then configure the agreed names and groups. Advanced settings or updates may still need the lighting manufacturer's app. See ecosystems and compatibility.

Who completes the electrical installation?

An appropriately registered electrical contractor must undertake fixed electrical installation work and its applicable testing or certification. CMAXX handles the agreed smart-device configuration and network integration. The proposal identifies the responsibility and costs rather than silently including or excluding electrical work.

Can I begin with one room and expand later?

Yes, where suitable compatible equipment is chosen and future requirements are considered. Begin with a useful room or circuit group and document the setup. Later expansion depends on network capacity, controller limits and ongoing product support, so compatibility should be rechecked for new additions.

Can you troubleshoot smart lights I already own?

We can assess supported devices and separate power, connectivity, app and routine issues. Share the brand, model, app and what has changed. Do not send passwords or open electrical fittings. Hardware failure or fixed-wiring faults may need manufacturer or electrical-contractor assistance. Use Smart Home Setup & Troubleshooting.
